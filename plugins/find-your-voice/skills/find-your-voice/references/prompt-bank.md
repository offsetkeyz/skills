# Prompt Bank

Contents: rules to state, prompts by bucket, adapting prompts, the taste test.

## Rules to state (two lines)

Write fast, 150-300 words, no editing, no AI. Voice dictation is fine. Raw is the point.

## Choosing order

1. Start with the Warm-up bucket (low stakes, gets them talking).
2. Then Opinionated or Work-facing, whichever matches what they'll publish.
3. Finish by filling gaps. Playful/Story is the best test of humor and personality, so use it once the profile has a base.

Cover at least three buckets before synthesizing.

## Warm-up (natural rhythm, no pressure)

1. Explain what you do to a smart friend at a bar.
2. Describe a typical Tuesday, including the annoying parts.
3. What's something you learned the hard way that you wish someone had told you earlier?
4. Describe your workspace or tools to someone who's never seen them.

## Casual / unfiltered (how they sound when relaxed or annoyed)

5. Rant about something in your field that wastes everyone's time. No job titles, no process words. Name the actual annoying thing.
6. Tell a story from earlier in your life that still comes up in conversation.
7. Defend something you like that other people think is a waste of time or money.
8. Describe the worst piece of advice you've received.

## Opinionated (blog and social core)

9. What do most people get wrong about your line of work?
10. Hot take: what's overhyped and what's real in your field right now?
11. One lesson from a career or life change, written for someone about to make the same move.
12. Argue against something you used to believe.

## Work-facing (calm, factual register)

13. Write the message announcing a process change to your team.
14. Explain how something you build or run works, start to finish, to a non-expert.
15. Give feedback to a colleague who did good work but communicated it badly.
16. Write the status update you'd send after a project slipped.

## Playful / Story (humor, imagery, personality)

17. Pitch something you care about in five sentences, no buzzwords.
18. Describe a small disaster as if it were a nature documentary.
19. Write a review of a mundane object or place as if it were a luxury experience.
20. Tell a story where you were wrong and it turned out funny.

## Adapting prompts

- Swap field-specific words for the person's domain ("your line of work" becomes teaching, nursing, carpentry, sales).
- If they publish in one format, add a format constraint late in the process: "Write the opening paragraph of a LinkedIn post about X."
- Never use a prompt that asks for something they wouldn't write about. A forced topic produces a forced voice.

## Taste test (when someone can't or won't write from scratch)

Pick a neutral topic relevant to them. Write the same 60-80 word passage in five or six styles, each labeled with a letter:

- **Plain and direct**: short sentences, facts first.
- **Conversational and warm**: contractions, a human aside.
- **Dry wit**: understatement, one wry line.
- **Storyteller**: a specific scene with a time and a detail.
- **Analytical**: numbered effects, a plain conclusion.
- **Punchy contrarian**: a reframed question, short declaratives.

Say plainly that the passages are style demonstrations you wrote, not samples of their writing. Invented scenes are fine here because they illustrate style, but never carry their content into the profile or into drafts.

Ask for: top one or two letters (or a blend), anything that felt wrong, and which sounds closest to how they'd say it out loud. Treat the answer as a hypothesis and confirm it against at least two raw samples before it goes into a profile.
